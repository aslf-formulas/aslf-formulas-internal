## 1. The Core Position

We are building a **longevity analyst** — the person people rely on to make
the entire field legible. Not a researcher, not a guru, not a protocol-seller.
The trusted weekly correspondent for longevity: fast, honest, and clear.

**Canonical identity statement** (use verbatim in the header of every repo
document that touches brand, content, or strategy, so the footprint is
consistent):

> *ASLF Formulas is the work of a longevity analyst who cares about the field
> so much that he built a clean product line he'd actually trust. The editorial
> mission leads; the products exist because of it, not the other way around.*

The order in that sentence is the entire strategy. Conviction came first. The
products are the proof of conviction, not the reason for the content.

---

## 2. The Origin Sequence (state it; never hide it)

The trust-killer in this space is the suspicion that the content exists to
serve the sale. Our defense is the truth: the caring came first, and the
products are downstream of it.

- We never pretend we don't sell anything. We make the **sequence** legible:
  cared → studied the field → couldn't find products clean enough → built them.
- This origin is told **once, properly**, as a dedicated asset, then sits
  quietly in the background as the context behind everything else.
- The substance must be real for this to hold — clean formulations, ingredient
  transparency, no filler. It is. That's why we can say it plainly.

---

## 3. What We Are NOT

- **Not competing with credentialed researchers** (Sinclair, Attia, etc.) on
  expertise or primary research. They generate and interpret studies; we don't
  challenge them on their turf and would lose if we tried.
- **Not a guru** selling a personal protocol or a fixed "stack."
- **Not a brand that makes content.** We are an editorial mission that happens
  to have built products. This distinction is the whole game — see §1.
- **Not a hype channel.** We don't overclaim, and we say so when something is
  just mice, just preliminary, or just doesn't work.

---

## 4. The Differentiator: Connective Analysis (the map, not the list)

Most longevity content is a **list**: here's study A, here's study B. Our edge
is the **map**: study A + this funding round + that policy shift point at the
same underlying trend — here's what it *means together*.

- This connective synthesis is rare and hard, which is exactly why it's
  defensible.
- It is the reason we cover the **industry** (funding, trials, IPOs, sovereign
  investment, policy, regulation), not just the science. Almost nobody else
  covers longevity as an industry with money and players. We do.
- "Eliminating noise" doesn't only mean cutting hype — it means cutting
  **disconnection**: the field throws fifty fragments a week at people and
  nobody tells them how they relate or which three actually matter. That's us.

Give the transition a few weeks; don't jerk either audience around.

## 6. The Content Split (the ratio is the message)

Output divides into two kinds, and the proportion *is* the trust signal:

- **Analyst content (the large majority):** news, explainers, connective
  theses — including many topics we sell nothing for (senescence, rapamycin
  trials, fasting, the funding landscape). When most of what we make has nothing
  to sell, the audience concludes *on its own* that we're here for the field.
- **Product content (the minority, clearly fenced):** its own mode, never
  smuggled into a piece that pretended to be education.

**Target ratio:** product-led pieces should be a clear minority of analyst-handle
output. *(Owner to set the exact number — proposed starting point: no more than
~1 in 5 analyst-handle pieces is product-led. Tune with data.)*

Selling is **fenced and subordinate**, never disguised as objectivity.
Audiences respect clear selling far more than they forgive selling dressed up
as analysis.

## 7. Editorial Principles

1. **Radical honesty in our own category.** We are the one who tells the truth
   about hair, skin, and supplements — *including the unflattering parts.* Say
   when a popular ingredient is underwhelming; explain real tradeoffs; flag when
   evidence is only mice. Acknowledging the limits of what supplements can do
   makes people trust our products **more**, not less.

2. **Anti-hype is the brand, not a constraint.** The compliance discipline we
   already run — qualifying mouse studies, citing sources, refusing to
   overclaim — is our **editorial signature**, not just legal cover. "The one
   who won't oversell you" is a moat in a space full of people who do.
   *(This reframes how AGT-018 should be understood: it protects the brand
   identity, not only FTC/platform exposure.)*

3. **Synthesist voice.** Clear, mature, science-literate, accessible. Reference
   points: Veritasium, Huberman. Never vlogger, never hype-merchant.

4. **Appointment franchises build the habit.** Being "the voice" means being a
   **habit**. Recurring, named, recognizable franchises (the weekly news first
   among them) turn viewers into "I check his thing every week." Protect the
   signature open. A format becomes a *show*.

## 8. The Beachhead

It's easier to be #1 in a narrow niche than #1 in all of longevity — so we
have a primary anchor, but the analyst identity covers the **full field** from
day one.

- **Hair science is the primary anchor, not the boundary.** It's the entry
  point new people find us through, and the topic we go deepest on — long-form
  hair content already lands, and "the person who actually understands the
  hair-science" is a winnable title this year.
- The rest of longevity (senescence, metabolic health, the funding/industry
  landscape, protocols, policy) is covered alongside it, with hair as the
  recurring deep-dive anchor rather than the only subject.
- Win the narrow title first; expand outward from earned credibility rather
  than trying to own all of longevity at once.
- Long-form is where authority compounds (connective theses prove depth); the
  weekly news is the recurring habit; shorts are reach that feeds both.

## 9. How We Measure It

"Voice of the field" is not raw subscriber count. Watch the habit-and-trust
signals:

| Signal | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Branded search** (name + "longevity") | People seeking *us* by name = becoming the anchor |
| **Repeat viewership** on the franchise | The habit is forming |
| **Share rate** on the news | People use us to keep *others* current |
| **Comment sentiment** ("I trust you / I come here for this") | Trust, not just reach |

This is a multi-year, compounding game. The mechanism is relentless
consistency — not missing, for a long time.
