## Teleprompter Script (clean read)

The 3 biggest things in longevity this week — plus a bonus. Let's get into it.

Number 1. A weight-loss drug just slowed biological aging in people — not mice, people. In a trial of around a hundred adults, those getting weekly semaglutide — the GLP-1 drug behind Ozempic and Wegovy — slowed their pace of aging by about nine percent on a leading epigenetic clock. The improvements showed up across markers tied to the heart, liver, kidneys, brain, and inflammation. Researchers think much of it traces back to losing visceral fat — the deep belly fat that drives inflammation as we age. Early — but it's human data.

Number 2. A few weeks back, we covered a startup that raised four hundred thirty-five million dollars to reprogram aging cells. The money is still pouring in. Retro Biosciences — backed by OpenAI's Sam Altman — has now been valued at one point eight billion dollars. Its stated goal: add ten healthy years to human life, using cellular reprogramming and the body's own recycling system. Its first human trial, a pill aimed at Alzheimer's biology, is already underway, with early data expected this summer. The reprogramming race has serious money behind it now.

Number 3. Your liver may quietly reveal how long you'll live. Researchers built a "liver age" score from a routine blood test plus two quick scans, then tested it on tens of thousands of people. It predicted who was at higher risk of dying better than their actual age did. A faster-aging liver tracked with higher risk across the board. It's a reminder that this one organ shapes how the whole body ages — and that you might be able to measure it early.

And your bonus. Our favorite ageless animal is back — the naked mole-rat. We've talked about their cancer resistance and their strangely youthful skin. New research adds another piece: their resting metabolism barely changes as they age. While most animals slow down and break down over time, theirs stays remarkably steady — part of why these little rodents live for decades with almost no signs of aging. Nature already solved a problem we're still chasing.

That's your longevity week. Want this every week? Follow. Drop your questions below — I read them all. See you next week.

**Story 2 — Retro Biosciences $1.8B valuation**
Overlay: *Source: STAT, May 22, 2026 / Longevity.Technology*
Full reference: Retro Biosciences, Sam Altman-backed; initial close at $1.8B pre-money valuation (led by 4P Capital). Programs in cellular reprogramming, autophagy (RTR242 — oral autophagy-boosting candidate, Phase 1, Alzheimer's-related biology), iPSC-derived cell therapies, and plasma-inspired therapeutics. Early trial data expected ~August 2026. Valuation announced May 22, 2026 (STAT; Retro blog); re-featured Fight Aging! June 15, 2026.
https://www.statnews.com/2026/05/22/retro-biosciences-longevity-valuation/
*Note: valuation is from late May — narration uses "has now been valued," no "this week" claim.*

**Story 3 — Liver Aging Index**
Overlay: *Source: Fight Aging!, June 22, 2026*
Full reference: Liver Aging Index (LAI) developed in China Kadoorie Biobank (N=21,629; clinical + plasma + imaging biomarkers), externally validated in NHANES (N=3,412) and VCTE-Prognosis (N=12,170). AUROC 0.764 / 0.759 for all-cause mortality, outperforming chronological age; each 1 SD of liver-aging acceleration linked to 22–85% higher all-cause mortality risk. Covered in Fight Aging! Newsletter, June 22, 2026.
https://www.fightaging.org/archives/2026/06/a-liver-aging-clock-predicts-all-cause-mortality/

**Bonus — Naked mole-rat metabolism**
Overlay: *Source: Fight Aging!, June 22, 2026*
Full reference: Study of resting metabolic rate (RMR) in naked mole-rats; body mass the primary predictor; absolute RMR substantially below mammals of similar size; no significant age effect on RMR once body mass accounted for — consistent with negligible-senescence phenotype. Covered in Fight Aging! Newsletter, June 22, 2026.
https://www.fightaging.org/archives/2026/06/naked-mole-rats-exhibit-a-consistently-low-resting-metabolic-rate-with-aging/
