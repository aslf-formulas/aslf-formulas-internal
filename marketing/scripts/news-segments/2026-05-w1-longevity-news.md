[STORY 2 — 0:40–1:17]
**A forgotten organ might predict how long you live**

"Story number two. A forgotten organ might predict how long you live. The thymus sits behind your breastbone, and for decades, doctors thought the thymus stopped mattering after puberty. A wave of new attention this week, including a Nature Biotechnology piece, says otherwise. Two Mass General Brigham studies used AI to score thymic health from routine CT scans of more than 25,000 adults. The highest scores: about 50% lower all-cause mortality, 63% lower cardiovascular death, and 36% lower lung cancer risk. That so-called useless organ may be quietly running the show."

[STORY 3 — 1:17–1:46]
**Why some brains never get dementia even when they're full of Alzheimer's**

"Story number three. Why do some brains never get dementia even when they're full of Alzheimer's? About 30% of older adults whose brains show full Alzheimer's pathology never developed the symptoms. A new study analyzed donated brains from the Netherlands Brain Bank and found a clue: adult neurogenesis. Resilient brains had immature neurons running active survival programs, with lower inflammation and less cell-death signaling. The repair crew is still showing up, and that may be what's saving them."

[STORY 4 — 1:46–2:13]
**Air pollution is aging your cells faster**

"Story number four. Air pollution isn't just bad for your lungs — it's literally aging your cells faster. A new UK Biobank analysis traced how it gets to your brain. People with the highest exposure to PM 2.5 had a 14% higher dementia risk. Nitrogen dioxide raised it 20%. The pathway runs through accelerated biological aging measured by epigenetic clocks, plus a higher senescent cell burden and changes to brain structure. The air is doing damage at the cellular level."

**A brand-new senolytic mechanism just cracked open COPD**

"Last story, number five. Last week, senolytic news was about which old drug works best. This week, a brand new mechanism just cracked open COPD. A molecule called BCL-xL PROTAC. Instead of blocking the protein keeping the senescent cells alive, it hijacks the cell's own degradation system to destroy that protein. In COPD lung tissue, it selectively killed senescent cells, left the healthy ones alone, and triggered new cell proliferation. The senolytic toolkit just got another precision weapon."
