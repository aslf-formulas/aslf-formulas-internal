Number two, a recent review covered by Fight Aging explains why damaged cells build up as we age. Throughout life, some cells become damaged and stop dividing. When we are young, the immune system removes them. With age, this cleanup ability weakens, so the cells accumulate and create ongoing low-level inflammation. What's clearer now is how much the aging immune system itself is responsible for letting them pile up.

And number three. A recent New Scientist article, based on work from researchers at University College London, highlights a surprising problem with the lights. Most of us live under LEDs. Red and near-infrared light helps the tiny power plants inside our cells make energy better. Modern LED lights and certain windows remove most of those wavelengths, researchers say. We may now be missing about 95% of the light spectrum. Our bodies evolved with this. This change in indoor lighting, and its possible effect on cellular energy, is only now being widely discussed.
