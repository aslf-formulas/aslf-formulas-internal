[STORY 1 — 0:06–0:36]
**Why bowhead whales live two hundred years cancer-free**

"Number 1. Bowhead whales live over two hundred years and almost never get cancer. They shouldn't — bigger bodies mean more cells, more chances for mutations to turn malignant. A new commentary published this week explains how they pull it off. It's not extra tumor-suppressor genes — that's how elephants do it. Whales evolved DNA repair so good their genome stays clean for two centuries. The question now: can we borrow that machinery."

---

[STORY 2 — 0:36–1:02]
**The first human mitochondrial transplants just happened**

"Number 2. Remember last week we covered SRN-901 — the multi-pathway combo, one pathway being mitophagy. This week a company called Mitrix Bio went further. They reported the first preliminary safety data on human mitochondrial transplants — bioreactor-grown mitochondria, infused into older patients. No adverse effects. Literally swapping in new power plants for your cells. It's already happening."

---

[STORY 3 — 1:02–1:26]
**A cheap senolytic combo just beat the expensive one head-to-head**

"Number 3. One of the first direct senolytic comparisons just dropped — dasatinib plus quercetin versus navitoclax, in laboratory studies of disc degeneration. The cheap off-label combo won decisively. Less senescence, less inflammation, less fibrosis. Navitoclax delivered none of it. The drugs that started this whole field still beat the newer ones."

---

[STORY 4 — 1:26–1:54]
**A peptide your body loses with age beat the standard arthritis drug in mice**

"Number 4. Your body makes a peptide called PEPITEM that puts the brakes on inflammation. Levels drop with age — part of why arthritis worsens over time. This week, researchers injected synthetic PEPITEM into arthritic mice. It beat infliximab — the current standard of care — across the board. Less swelling. Less cartilage damage. Less bone erosion. Replacing what your body has lost beat blocking what it's overproducing."

---

[STORY 5 — 1:54–2:16]
**The bacteria in your gut are tracking your strength after sixty-five**

"Number 5. A new study on adults sixty-five and up found specific gut microbes correlate tightly with physical strength and activity — even after controlling for diet. Prevotella and Roseburia — more activity, more strength. Bilophila and Eggerthella — the opposite. Same diet, different microbes, different bodies in old age."
