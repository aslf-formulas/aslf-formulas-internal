[STORY 1 — 0:04–0:32]
**Why bowhead whales live 200 years cancer-free**

"Number 1. Why bowhead whales live 200 years cancer-free. Bowhead whales live over 200 years and almost never get cancer. They shouldn't, really. Bigger bodies mean more cells, more chances for mutations to turn malignant. A new commentary published this week explains how they pull it off. It's not extra tumor-suppressor genes — that's how elephants do it. Bowhead whales evolved DNA repair so good their genome stays clean for two centuries. The question now: can we borrow that machinery?"

---

[STORY 2 — 0:32–1:00]
**The first human mitochondrial transplants just happened**

"Number 2. The first human mitochondrial transplants just happened. Remember last week we covered SRN-901 — the multi-pathway combo, one pathway being mitophagy. This week, a company called Mitrix Bio went further. They reported the first preliminary safety data on human mitochondrial transplants — bioreactor-grown mitochondria infused into older patients. No adverse effects. Literally swapping in new power plants for your cells. It's already happening."

---

[STORY 3 — 1:00–1:26]
**A cheap senolytic combo just beat the expensive one head-to-head**

"Number 3. A cheap senolytic combo just beat the expensive one head-to-head. One of the first direct senolytic comparisons just dropped — dasatinib plus quercetin versus navitoclax, in laboratory studies of disc degeneration. The cheap off-label combo won decisively. Less senescence, less inflammation, less fibrosis. Navitoclax delivered none of it. The drugs that started this whole field still beat the newer ones."

---

[STORY 4 — 1:26–1:57]
**A peptide your body loses with age beat the standard arthritis drug in mice**

"Number 4. A peptide your body loses with age beat the standard arthritis drug in mice. Your body makes a peptide called PEPITEM that puts the brakes on inflammation. Levels drop with age — part of why arthritis worsens over time. This week, researchers injected synthetic PEPITEM into arthritic mice. It beat infliximab — which is the current standard of care — across the board for rheumatoid arthritis. Less swelling, less cartilage damage, less bone erosion. That means replacing what your body has lost is beating blocking what it's overproducing."

---

[STORY 5 — 1:57–2:21]
**The bacteria in your gut are tracking your strength after sixty-five**

"And number 5. The bacteria in your gut are predicting your strength after 65. A new study on adults 65 and up found specific gut microbes correlate tightly with physical strength and activity, even after controlling for diet. Prevotella and Roseburia — more activity, more strength. Bilophila and Eggerthella, the opposite. Same diet, different microbes, different bodies in old age."
