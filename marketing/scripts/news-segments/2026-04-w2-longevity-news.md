[STORY 1 — 0:08–0:45]
**The first senolytic drug just cleared Phase 1 — and it works through your skin**

"Senolytics are drugs that kill the 'zombie' cells that accumulate with age and drive inflammation, tissue damage, and accelerated aging. A biotech called Rubedo just ran a Phase 1 trial of a topical senolytic — RLS-1496 — in people with sun-damaged skin lesions. It passed safety, and early signals show it's actually clearing senescent cells. Why this matters: it's the furthest a targeted senolytic has moved in humans. Skin is just the entry point. The same class is being developed for joints, lungs, and the aging brain. This is the leading edge of an entirely new category of medicine."

[STORY 2 — 0:45–1:15]
**Your genes matter less than we thought — a lot less**

"For years, the working assumption was that genetics controlled roughly 20 percent of how long you live. A new analysis just revised that estimate significantly — closer to 50-50. Genes and environment, roughly tied. That's a fundamental shift. It means sleep, movement, diet, stress, and social connection have nearly twice the leverage we were crediting them with. If you've ever written yourself off as having bad genes, the science no longer backs that up."

---

[STORY 3 — 1:15–1:50]
**Three daily habits tied to 9 extra years of life**

"A new study put a number on it. Nail three specific habits consistently and researchers linked it to a 9.35-year increase in both lifespan and healthspan — the years you're actually healthy, not just alive. Here they are: 7.2 hours of sleep. 42 minutes of physical activity per day. And a diet quality score of at least 58 — whole foods, more fiber, significantly less ultra-processed junk. No supplement, no drug, no biohack currently in development comes close to that return on investment."

[STORY 4 — 1:50–2:20]
**Life Biosciences just raised $80 million to run the first-ever epigenetic reprogramming trial in humans**

"That funding will complete their Phase 1 trial of ER-100 — targeting optic neuropathies like glaucoma and optic nerve damage — and push their Partial Epigenetic Reprogramming platform into new tissues. The trial is already underway. Here's why this is significant: the eye is one of the hardest tissues to regenerate. If reprogramming works there, it's evidence this isn't a niche application — it's a general-purpose tool for reversing cellular aging anywhere in the body."

---

[STORY 5 — 2:20–2:50]
**The biggest NAD+ consensus review in years just dropped — and the answer is complicated**

"Twenty-five of the world's top NAD+ researchers published a consensus paper in Nature Aging. The verdict: NAD+ precursors like NR and NMN do reliably raise NAD+ levels in humans. Biochemically, they work. But on hard clinical endpoints — muscle strength, cognition, metabolic health — the results are genuinely mixed. Some trials show benefit. Many show nothing. The honest translation: NAD+ isn't a scam, but it's not a silver bullet either. The field needs larger, longer trials. If you're supplementing, one pathway alone has limits — and anyone telling you otherwise isn't reading the same literature."
