**First patient in a Phase 3 gene therapy for aging vision loss**

"Number 1. A company just crossed into the last big human test. Ocugen dosed the first patient in a Phase 3 trial of OCU410 — a one-time gene therapy aimed at the late-stage vision loss that comes with dry age-related macular degeneration. Two hundred thirty-seven people. First pivotal gene-therapy trial for that condition. The FDA already gave it a regenerative-medicine fast track called RMAT. This is not a finished medicine. [STORY 2 — 0:48–1:24]
**Dogs and humans share blood clues to longevity**

"Number 2. Your dog might be teaching us how humans age. The Dog Aging Project looked at blood metabolites in about nine hundred thirty-seven pet dogs — animals that share our homes, food patterns, and daily stress, not lab cages. Patterns tied to shorter or longer canine life closely matched patterns from large human mortality studies. Metabolism, inflammation, cellular stress — same fingerprints. Dogs age faster than we do, so researchers can validate aging biomarkers in years instead of decades. This is not a claim that dog blood makes you live longer. It is a human-relevant map of what death risk looks like in the bloodstream."

[ON-SCREEN: "Companion dogs · Journals of Gerontology · Aug 2026"]

---

[STORY 3 — 1:24–1:58]
**Your organs don’t age on one clock**

"Number 3. Your organs do not age on one shared clock. A Sanford Burnham team used AI on about twenty-five thousand tissue images from nearly a thousand people, across forty organs. They found three schedules: some tissues speed up early — especially blood vessels in the thirties — some speed up later, and some hit two waves. When those accelerations hit, the shared signature is more inflammation and less energy and repair. Aging is not one number on your wrist. It is a set of organs moving on different clocks, sometimes in sync."
