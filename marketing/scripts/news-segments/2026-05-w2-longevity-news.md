[STORY 1 — 0:06–0:37]
**Why naked mole-rats have youthful skin at 40 years old**

"Number 1. Why naked mole-rats have youthful skin at 40 years old. They live up to 40 years. Their skin barely ages. A new paper this week mapped their skin tissue molecule by molecule. The hyaluronic acid doesn't sit evenly the way it does in ours — it forms tight clusters, packed right under the basement membrane. And while mouse skin thins with age, naked mole-rat skin gets thicker. It's not how much hyaluronic acid is there. It's how it's organized."

---

[STORY 2 — 0:37–1:13]
**Greenland sharks live 300 years with hearts that look damaged**

"Number 2. Greenland sharks live 300 years with hearts that look damaged. A new study this week is the first to look at the cellular biochemistry of aging in these sharks. Their hearts show every classic hallmark of aging — extensive fibrosis, massive lipofuscin buildup, damaged mitochondria, oxidative stress markers everywhere. By every measure on the page, these hearts should be failing. And yet at capture, the sharks were physiologically healthy. The takeaway — in the longest-lived vertebrates, longevity isn't about avoiding damage. It's about being resilient to it."

---

[STORY 3 — 1:13–1:43]
**In people over 90, lifestyle outweighs longevity genes by three to one**

"Number 3. In people over 90, lifestyle outweighs longevity genes by three to one. A new study in npj Aging tracked the oldest-old Han Chinese population. A favorable modifiable risk profile — eleven lifestyle and health factors — cut mortality risk by 40.7 percent. Genetic predisposition to long life only cut it 13 percent. And here's the kicker. An unfavorable lifestyle profile largely erased the genetic advantage. Even at 90, what you do still wins."

---

[STORY 4 — 1:43–2:16]
**A country just turned itself into a longevity testing ground**

"Number 4. A country just turned itself into a longevity testing ground. At the Milken Institute Global Conference this month, Abu Dhabi's Department of Health publicly invited longevity companies to build and test technologies across the entire emirate — hospitals, ambulances, clinics, the whole national health network wired together. Most longevity tech sits trapped in isolated pilots. This is the first attempt to integrate geroscience into a functioning national system at scale. If it works, expect other countries to copy it fast."

---

[STORY 5 — 2:16–2:54]
**A two-drug combo extended lifespan in old male mice by around 70 percent**

"And number 5. A two-drug combo extended lifespan in old, frail mice by around 70 percent — but only in the males. A new Aging-US cover paper out of UC Berkeley paired oxytocin with an Alk5 inhibitor. Treated old male mice got stronger, healthier, and lived dramatically longer. The treatment restored youthful blood protein patterns and hit the pathways that drive tissue decline. Female mice — only short-term improvements. Same drugs, same dose, completely different outcome. One more data point that male and female aging may need different therapies."

---
