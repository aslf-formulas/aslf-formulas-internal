[STORY 1 — 0:06–0:44]
**A world leader got caught on a hot mic talking about living forever**

"Number 1. A world leader got caught on a hot mic talking about living forever. At a military parade this month, an open microphone caught Russian President Vladimir Putin telling China's Xi Jinping that humans could keep replacing organs — and maybe even become immortal. According to the Wall Street Journal, it's not just idle talk. It's a state-backed national longevity program — bioprinting living tissue, growing organs inside animal hosts, gene therapy aimed at slowing aging, with a goal of replacement organs by 2030. The catch? Russian scientists say almost none of it has shown up in peer-reviewed journals. One called it 'aspirations, not results.' Bioprinting and growing organs in animals are real fields labs worldwide are chasing. But the standard's the same for a president as for anyone — show the published data."

---

[STORY 2 — 0:44–1:20]
**The same longevity drug may need to work differently in men and women**

"Number 2. The same longevity drug may need to work completely differently in men and women. Remember last week — that drug combo that extended lifespan, but only in the male mice? This week, a major review lays out why sex matters so much in aging. Women's immune systems carry extra copies of key immune genes and stay more active with age — stronger defense, but more autoimmune risk. Men lean harder on a different branch of immunity, and tend to fare worse against infection. The takeaway — one-size-fits-all aging medicine may be ending. The future probably looks different for each sex."

---

[STORY 3 — 1:20–1:54]
**For the first time, scientists safely pulled a root-cause molecule out of human arteries**

"Number 3. For the first time, scientists safely pulled a damaging molecule straight out of human arteries. A company called Cyclarity reported first-in-human safety data on a designed molecule that grabs onto 7-ketocholesterol — what researchers describe as a root cause of arterial plaque — and flushes it out of the body. In this early trial, it cleared the safety bar. Most heart drugs work on the whole system at once. This one's aimed at a single damaging molecule, right at the source. Early days — but this is real, published human data. Not a promise."

---

[STORY 4 — 1:54–2:21]
**A photo of the back of your eye might predict your bone health**

"Number 4. A photo of the back of your eye might predict the strength of your bones. Researchers used an AI tool that reads biological age from a single retinal scan — and across nearly 46,000 people, an older-looking retina was linked to lower bone density and higher fracture risk. The eye and the skeleton seem totally unrelated. But aging shows up across the whole body at the same time — and a quick, non-invasive eye scan may reveal what's happening in bones you can't see."

---

[STORY 5 — 2:21–2:54]
**A blood test may flag dementia risk years before symptoms**

"And number 5. A blood test may flag dementia risk years before any symptoms appear. In a UK Biobank analysis of more than 220,000 people, researchers built what's called a metabolomic clock from molecules in the blood. People whose blood looked biologically older had a higher risk of later dementia. And in those who also carried two copies of a known high-risk gene, the analysis showed roughly a tenfold higher risk. To be clear — this is an observational finding about risk, not a diagnosis. But it points toward catching trouble far earlier than we can today."
