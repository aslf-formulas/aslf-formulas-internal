**Tweet 2 — BioAge Labs / BG-102** *(native clip — strongest):*
BioAge Labs dosed the first patient in a Phase 2 trial of BG-102, an oral drug that blocks a key inflammation pathway. What makes it different: they found the target by studying people who age more slowly — not by starting with a single disease and working backward. Most drugs are built the disease-first way. This one was designed from longevity data.

**Tweet 3 — NewLimit scale-up:**
NewLimit reported a major manufacturing scale-up of its lead reprogramming medicine, plus new data showing the same approach restores youthful function in old liver cells and the cells lining blood vessels. First human trials are still targeted for 2027. Scaling manufacturing is one of the least-discussed but biggest barriers between a lab result and a real human study.

**Tweet 4 — APOE2 mechanism (Buck Institute):**
A Buck Institute study shows that the APOE2 gene variant — long associated with longer life — helps neurons repair DNA damage and resist becoming senescent. For years the longevity link was a statistical observation. This study shows the cellular mechanism behind it: better DNA maintenance in the neurons themselves. A concrete target for future work to try to replicate.

**Tweet 5 — Genetic aging map (in mice — final tweet):**
A large Nature study in mice mapped 29 genetic regions that influence mortality risk — and found that the genes that matter most in one life stage are largely different from those that matter later. In mice, the timing of which biological processes are most influential shifts across the lifespan. If the pattern holds in people, when you intervene may matter as much as how.
